<Callout type="info" title="Growth is a system">
Recruitment fills slots. Retention builds an alliance. The work is mostly boring: clear expectations, reliable routines, fair recognition, documented decisions.
</Callout>

## The one-page charter

Every alliance needs a document a new member can read in five minutes:

- **Schedule:** reset time, event windows, voice windows.
- **Expectations:** minimum participation, communication, spend discipline.
- **Roles:** who calls what, who to ask.
- **Culture:** how mistakes are handled, how contributions are recognised.
- **Links:** wiki, Discord, calendar.

If the charter takes more than a page, it will not be read.

## Roles that prevent failure

| Role | Responsibility | Backup required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commander | Final decisions, diplomacy | Yes |
| Phase captain | Target, spend call, final push | Yes |
| Recruiter | Pipeline, onboarding | No |
| Archivist | Screenshots, after-action notes | Yes |
| Analyst | Scoring ledger, conversion rates | No |

<Callout type="strategy" title="Two-deep leadership">
Every critical role needs a second person. Single-point leadership is the most common cause of a collapsed event week.
</Callout>

## Contribution paths

Raw score is the easiest metric to track and the worst one to use alone. Offer roles for different accounts:

- **High power:** rally lead, frontline combat, contested objective.
- **Mid power:** reinforcement, secondary objective, phase co-captain.
- **Low power:** scouting, logistics, documentation, defence rotation.
- **Non-combat:** scheduling, recruitment, wiki upkeep.

A member who scouts for every Raid phase is contributing. Say so publicly.

## Communication routine

A predictable rhythm reduces anxiety and duplicate work:

- **Sunday:** publish the week plan.
- **Daily reset:** confirm the phase and target.
- **Mid-phase:** one status update with the score delta.
- **Final hour:** closer call.
- **Next day:** short after-action note.

One channel, one pinned plan, one owner. More channels create more noise, not more coordination.

## Handling a losing week

Losing is data. Run a 15-minute review:

1. What was the target?
2. What did we actually score per phase?
3. Where did participation drop?
4. Was the plan clear?
5. What is one change for next week?

Do not turn the review into a blame session. The goal is one process improvement, not a scapegoat.
