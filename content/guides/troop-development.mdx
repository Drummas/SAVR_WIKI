## The throughput rule

Troops are produced by time. If the queue is idle, you are losing troops you could have had for free. The first optimisation is not a better tier; it is **never leaving the queue empty**.

A sustainable rhythm looks like:

1. Log in.
2. Claim completed batch.
3. Start the next batch sized to finish before the next login.
4. Use alliance help.
5. Log off.

Only after that baseline is stable should you optimise composition.

## Tier discipline

Higher tiers are not automatically better value. Consider:

- **Support systems:** research, hero buffs and equipment that affect the tier.
- **Resource cost:** can your economy sustain a full batch?
- **Healing cost:** can you replace losses after a fight?
- **Event requirements:** some scoring actions care about training volume, not tier.
- **Defence:** a smaller high-tier army on the map leaves the base exposed.

<Callout type="strategy" title="Tier coverage beats tier purity">
A mixed army that keeps your base defended, your events scored and your rallies filled is usually stronger than a perfect single-tier army you cannot sustain.
</Callout>

## Reading a battle report

Look for patterns, not one number:

| Signal | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Front line dies early | Hero/troop tank gap, or bad matchup |
| High damage but low kills | Overkill, target selection or squishy carry |
| Losses spike after round 1 | Sustain or healing problem |
| Win with heavy losses | Not sustainable for Raid phase |
| Loss with favorable delta | Composition may still be improving |

Capture the report and tag it with the patch, opponent type and composition. A battle report without context is just a screenshot.

## Preparation for AvA

Training speed-ups are a scored resource in the Preparation phase (community-reported). Keep a reserve, but do not train troops you would not otherwise want. Score should follow a useful army, not create one you cannot heal.
